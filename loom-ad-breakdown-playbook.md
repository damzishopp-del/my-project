# Loom Ad Breakdown Playbook
### A plug-and-play framework for single-creative video breakdowns (Meta Ads · CBO · Cold Traffic)

**Purpose:** Record a 6–10 minute Loom that breaks down ONE ad, delivers real value for free, and positions you as the media buyer / creative strategist who runs this system.
**Rule #1:** Value first. No pitch until the final 30 seconds, and even then it is a soft one.
**Tone:** Direct. Confident. Unpretentious. Analytical. Peer-to-peer, never guru-to-student.

### Data to Pull From Ads Manager (have these on a second screen or in a doc)

- [ ] **Spend** on this ad (lifetime and per window). This is your headline metric.
- [ ] **Spend share** vs. other ads in the same ad set / campaign (shows how much CBO favored it).
- [ ] **Hook rate**: 3-sec video plays ÷ impressions.
- [ ] **Hold rate**: ThruPlays (or 15-sec plays) ÷ 3-sec plays.
- [ ] **CTR (link)**, **CPM**, **CPC**.
- [ ] **CVR** (click → purchase / lead) and **CPA / ROAS**.
- [ ] **Retention curve**: where the drop-offs are (the video-view graph in Ads Manager).
- [ ] **Time live** and **frequency**, to separate "fatigued" from "never took off."

> **Honesty check:** Only show numbers you can show. If the data is confidential, blur it, round it, or use ratios (e.g., "3.2x the spend of the next ad in the ad set"). Never fabricate or inflate. The whole play depends on credibility.

### Choosing the Right Ad to Break Down
- **Best pick (winning):** An ad with meaningful spend on cold traffic that stayed profitable. Clear angle, clean to explain.
- **Best pick (non-winning):** An ad that got **real budget** (so Meta gave it a fair shot) and failed to hold efficiency. Avoid ads that never got spend. See the note in Section 2.
- **Relevance:** Prefer an ad in the **same vertical** as the person you are sending it to.
- **One ad per Loom.** Depth beats breadth.

## Section 1: The Hook (0:00–0:20)

**Goal:** In under 20 seconds, the viewer knows (1) what this is, (2) it is real data, (3) what they will walk away with.
**Rules:** No "Hey guys, hope you're doing well." No intro about yourself. Start with the statement.

**On screen:** Face cam (bubble) over the ad's static thumbnail or first frame. Show the spend number early if you can.

### Option A: Winning Ad Hook

> **[Spoken]**
> "Doing a breakdown of a **winning ad** we spent **[X amount]** on **profitably** for **[Brand Y]**. In this video, you'll see exactly how I analyze the ad, **why it drove profitable scale**, and how we **build iterations from it to make more winners**."

**Optional tightener (add one sentence max):**
> "Everything you're about to see is from the real ad account, real spend, nothing theoretical."

### Option B: Non-Winning Ad Hook

> **[Spoken]**
> "Doing a breakdown of a **non-winning ad** we spent **[X amount]** on **unprofitably** for **[Brand Y]**. In this video, you'll see **how I analyze where it failed**, and how we turn these insights into a **new iteration** for the next ad, to **capture budget in a CBO** and **scale profitably on cold traffic** over time on Meta Ads."

**Optional tightener:**
> "Losers are the cheapest education in paid social. Here's how I extract the lesson from this one."

### Hook Delivery Notes
- **Say the spend number clearly.** It is the credibility anchor.
- Keep your energy level **calm and matter-of-fact**. Confidence comes from specifics, not volume.
- Do not apologize for the loser. A non-winner you can explain is a **proof of process**, not a weakness.

## Section 2: The Data Standard (0:20–1:00)

**Goal:** Define success *before* analyzing anything, so the viewer accepts your verdict. Reframe "what is a good ad" away from opinions and toward Meta's behavior.

**On screen:** Ads Manager, campaign view, **sorted by spend (descending)**. Highlight the spend column with your cursor.

### Script

> **[Spoken]**
> "Before we look at the ad, one rule I use for everything: **Meta speaks through spend.**
>
> Inside a CBO, **Meta's algorithm decides where the budget goes.** It is constantly running a live auction-based test of every creative, and **spend is its vote.**
>
> **Spend equals winner, in Meta's eyes.** If an ad is getting real budget *and* the numbers hold up, that's a winner, and we scale it.
>
> **Low or no spend equals a loser that needs fixing.** Meta is telling us this creative isn't earning the right to the budget: not enough signal that people will stop, click, and buy.
>
> So in this video, I'm not going to tell you what I *think* about the ad. We're going to use **spend as the scoreboard**, then work backwards to figure out *why*."

**On-screen action:** Point to the ad's spend vs. other ads in the ad set. Say the ratio out loud: *"This ad got [X]% of the ad set's spend."*

### Nuance Box (use if you want to preempt pushback)

> **Spend alone is not profit.** Spend tells you Meta *likes* the creative. **Spend + efficiency (ROAS / CPA vs. your breakeven) tells you it's a true winner.** That's why the winning example above is specifically *profitable* spend.
>
> **Caveat on low-spend ads:** Low spend can also result from poor test structure (too many ads in one ad set, tiny budgets, or a recently launched ad). Before calling an ad a loser, confirm it was given a fair chance: similar launch window, no structural issue, and enough impressions to read the hook metrics.

### Definitions to Keep in Your Pocket

| Label | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Winner** | Gets a disproportionate share of spend in a CBO **and** hits or beats target efficiency (ROAS / CPA). |
| **Loser (fixable)** | Got spend, did not hold efficiency. Diagnose which stage failed (hook, hold, click, landing, offer). |
| **Dead on arrival** | Never got spend. Almost always a hook or relevance problem. Fix the first 3 seconds first. |

## Section 3: The Full Creative Pipeline (1:00–2:45)

**Goal:** Show that one ad is the *output of a system*, not luck. This is where you demonstrate depth and that you operate end-to-end.

**On screen:** A simple left-to-right 8-box diagram (Miro / Figma / Notion / even a hand-drawn slide). Highlight each step as you say it. **Spend roughly 12–15 seconds per step.**

```
┌──────────┐   ┌────────┐   ┌─────────┐   ┌──────────┐
│ 1.RESEARCH│ → │ 2.IDEA │ → │ 3.GROWTH│ → │4.SCRIPTING│
│ Market +  │   │ GENERATION│ │ PLAYBOOK │   │           │
│ Audience  │   │          │   │ PLANNING │   │           │
└──────────┘   └────────┘   └─────────┘   └──────────┘
                                                   ↓
┌──────────┐   ┌────────┐   ┌─────────┐   ┌──────────┐
│8.LEARN +  │ ← │7.DATA  │ ← │6.LAUNCH │ ← │5.PRODUCTION│
│ REPEAT    │   │ ANALYSIS│   │ IN CBO  │   │ / EDITING │
└──────────┘   └────────┘   └─────────┘   └──────────┘
```

### Opening Line for This Section

> **[Spoken]**
> "An ad that wins doesn't come from a lucky idea. It comes from a pipeline. Here's the exact pipeline this ad went through, and notice that **step 8 feeds back into step 1.** That's the whole game."

### Step-by-Step Script

#### Step 1: Research (Market and Audience)
> "It starts with research: **market research** (what competitors are running, what's saturated, what's missing) and **audience research** (reviews, comments, Reddit, support tickets, customer language). The goal is to find **the exact words customers use** for their problems and desires, and the **awareness level** we're speaking to on cold traffic."

- **Show:** One real example of a customer quote or review you pulled for `[Brand Y]`.
- **Say one specific:** `[Specific Insight, e.g., "Customers kept saying 'I've tried everything' → that became our angle."]`

#### Step 2: Idea Generation
> "From research we generate **ideas**, and I separate them into **angles** (the reason to care), **hooks** (how we stop the scroll), and **formats** (how it's delivered: UGC, founder, demo, static-to-video). One angle can spawn ten ads."

- **Show:** A simple idea bank / spreadsheet with angle → hook → format columns.

#### Step 3: Growth Playbook Planning
> "Then I plan **what we're testing and why**: which angles get tested first, which audiences' awareness levels they target, how many variations per concept, and what result tells us to scale versus kill. Without this step, you're launching ads. With it, you're running **experiments**."

- **Show:** Testing roadmap (concept, hypothesis, success metric, kill rule).
- **Key phrase:** *"Every ad launches with a hypothesis and a kill rule."*

#### Step 4: Scripting
> "Scripting is where the angle becomes a structure: **hook, problem, mechanism, proof, offer, CTA.** The first three seconds are scripted separately, because **the hook decides whether the rest of the ad even exists.**"

- **Show:** The actual script of this ad with the hook line highlighted.

#### Step 5: Creative Production / Editing
> "Production and editing: shot list, creator briefing, pacing, captions, sound, and **hook variations**. The same body can carry 3–5 different hooks, which is the cheapest way to find a winner."

- **Show:** Quick view of raw footage → final edit, or the hook variants side by side.

#### Step 6: Launching in a CBO Campaign
> "We launch in a **CBO**, with creatives grouped by **concept**, not randomly. We give Meta clean signal: consistent budget, enough creatives to compete, and **cold traffic** so we read true top-of-funnel performance. Then **Meta allocates the budget**, and that's the scoreboard from earlier."

- **Show:** The CBO structure in Ads Manager (campaign → ad sets → ads).

#### Step 7: Data Analysis
> "Then we **read the data in order of the funnel**: spend share, hook rate, hold rate, CTR, CVR, CPA / ROAS. Each metric points to one stage of the ad. That's how a loser tells you *exactly* what to fix."

- **Show:** The metric-to-stage map (see Appendix B).

#### Step 8: Learning and Repeating
> "Finally, the learning goes back into research and ideation. **Winners get iterated. Losers get diagnosed.** Both become the input for the next round. This loop is where compounding happens."

- **Show:** Arrow from step 8 back to step 1.

### Transition Line Into the Ad

> "Now that you've seen the system, let's open the ad and walk through it the way I would on any client account."

## Section 4: The Core Analysis (2:45–6:15)

**Goal:** Show structured, repeatable analytical competence. The viewer should think *"I could do this on my own ads tomorrow."*

**Structure:** Watch once uninterrupted → re-watch in **four lenses** → tie each lens to a metric.

**On screen:** Ad playing full-screen with sound **once** (15–30 sec), then paused/scrubbed for each lens. Keep the metrics panel visible in a split view or switch tabs.

### Step 0: Watch It Cold (0:20 of the section)

> "First, let me play it the way a customer would see it: scrolling, sound on."
> *(Play the ad. Don't talk over it.)*

### Lens 1: Hook Performance (the first 3 seconds)

**Questions you answer:** Does it stop the scroll? Is the hook visual, verbal, or both? Is it clear who this is for?

**On screen:** Scrub to seconds 0–3. Pause on the first frame.

> **[Spoken, Winning Ad]**
> "The hook here is a **[Hook Type]**. In the first three seconds, we get **[describe: visual + line]**. **Hook rate is [X]%**, which means [above / below] the benchmark I look for. It works because **[reason: it names the audience, creates curiosity, shows a pattern interrupt, makes a bold claim]**."
>
> **[Spoken, Non-Winning Ad]**
> "The hook here is a **[Hook Type]**, but look at the hook rate: **[X]%**. The opening frame **[describe]**, and I see three problems: **[1. It doesn't name who it's for. 2. The first line is slow / generic. 3. The visual doesn't interrupt the feed.]** That's where we lose people before the message even starts."

**Metrics to cite:** Hook rate (3-sec plays ÷ impressions), thumb-stop rate.
**Benchmarks (rules of thumb, adjust per account):** Hook rate ~25–35%+ is solid on cold traffic; under ~20% usually signals a hook problem.

### Lens 2: Messaging Angle

**Questions you answer:** What is the single idea? Who is it for? What awareness level does it speak to?

> "The **angle** is **[Angle]**. It speaks to someone who is **[problem-aware / solution-aware / product-aware]**. The core promise: **[one sentence]**. What makes this angle strong is **[Specific Insight, e.g., it uses customer language straight from reviews / it reframes the problem / it targets an objection]**."
>
> *(Non-winner)* "The angle is **[Angle]**, but it's **[too broad / too product-focused for cold traffic / solving a problem the audience doesn't feel urgently]**. On cold traffic, the audience doesn't know us, so **the angle has to earn attention before the product gets mentioned.**"

**Fast test to show on screen:** *"Can I say the angle in one sentence without the product name?"*

### Lens 3: Core Mechanism

**Questions you answer:** *Why* should the viewer believe this works? What is the "reason to believe"?

> "The **mechanism** is **[Mechanism]**. This is the 'why it works' that makes the promise believable. Without a mechanism, it's just a claim; with one, it's a **new reason to try something**, even for people who've been burned before."
>
> *(Non-winner)* "The mechanism isn't clear until **[timestamp]**, which is too late. Or it's the same mechanism every competitor in the space uses, so there's nothing new to believe."

**Check for:** Is it unique? Is it simple enough to explain in one breath? Is there proof (demo, visual, testimonial, data)?

### Lens 4: Visual Pacing and Structure

**Questions you answer:** Where does attention hold or drop? Does the visual rhythm match the message?

**On screen:** Show the **retention curve** next to the video timeline. Mark the drop-off points.

> "Looking at the retention curve, we hold well until **[timestamp]**, then drop. Right there on screen, **[what happens: a long talking-head stretch / slow transition / offer delay / no visual change for X seconds]**. The fix is a **pattern change every 2–4 seconds**: B-roll, text callout, zoom, or cut."
>
> *(Winner)* "Notice the pacing: a visual change roughly every **[X] seconds**, text reinforces the spoken line, and the **proof arrives at [timestamp]**, right before attention would normally dip."

**Check for:** Cut frequency, on-screen text, sound design, captions, proof placement, CTA clarity, and aspect ratio / safe zones.

### Closing the Analysis: The Verdict Slide

**On screen:** A one-slide summary.

| Lens | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌ | Hook rate `[X]%` |
| Angle | ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌ | `[Specific Insight]` |
| Mechanism | ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌ | `[Observation]` |
| Pacing | ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌ | Retention drop at `[timestamp]` |

> **[Spoken]**
> "So the verdict: **[Winner: all four lenses aligned, and the spend reflects it. / Non-winner: the real break was in __, everything else was solid, which is good news, because it means we iterate rather than start over.]**"

## Section 5: The Iteration Plan (6:15–7:45)

**Goal:** Prove that the analysis leads to **action**. This is the bridge from "interesting" to "I want this person running my account."

**On screen:** A simple iteration matrix (Notion / Sheets / slide).

### The Iteration Principle

> "**One ad gives us a hypothesis, not a conclusion.** Every iteration changes **one variable** so we know what caused the result."

### Iteration Ladder (use in this order)

| Level | What changes | When to use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Hook swap** | Only the first 3 seconds | Body is strong, but hook rate is low (or you want to push a winner further) | Same ad, 3 new hooks |
| **2. Angle variation** | Same mechanism, new angle / audience | Mechanism proved itself; test who else cares | Same product demo for a different problem |
| **3. Format change** | Same message, different format | Message works; test fatigue resistance | UGC → founder → static-to-video |
| **4. Mechanism / concept** | New "why it works" | Angle works but belief is the issue | New proof or unique-mechanism framing |
| **5. New concept** | Back to research | Multiple iterations failed | Return to Step 1 of the pipeline |

### Script: Winner Version

> "Because this ad won, we don't redo it, we **extend it**. Here's the iteration plan:
> **1.** Three new hooks on the same body, to push the ad's reach and beat fatigue.
> **2.** Same mechanism with a new angle: **[Iteration Idea]**.
> **3.** A format variation: **[format]**.
> The goal isn't one winner. It's **a family of winners from one insight**, so the account doesn't depend on a single creative."

### Script: Non-Winner Version

> "Because the failure point was **[stage]**, the fix is surgical. The iteration:
> **1.** Keep **[what worked]**.
> **2.** Change **[the one broken variable]** to **[specific change]**.
> **3.** Launch it in the same CBO so it competes for budget directly.
> If Meta starts giving it spend and the efficiency holds, we've turned a loser into a winner without wasting the learning."

### Show a Mini Example

Put the **before → after** side by side (original hook vs. new hook text, or original script line vs. rewritten line). Seeing the actual change makes this concrete.

## Section 6: Pure Value and Authority CTA (7:45–9:00)

**Goal:** Give them a checklist they can implement **tomorrow morning**, then close with a low-pressure invitation. The value must feel complete even if they never reply.

### Part A: The "Tomorrow Morning" Action List (45–60 sec)

**On screen:** A 5-bullet checklist. Read it slowly and let it sit on screen.

> **[Spoken]**
> "If you take nothing else from this video, here's what you can do **tomorrow morning**, no agency, no new tools:
>
> **1. Sort your CBO by spend.** Whatever Meta is giving budget to is your current winner list. Write down what those ads have in common.
>
> **2. Pull hook rate on your last 10 ads.** Anything under about 20% has a first-3-seconds problem. Don't touch the body, just rewrite the opening and relaunch it.
>
> **3. Write the angle and mechanism of each ad in one sentence.** If you can't, the viewer can't either.
>
> **4. Take your top winner and make 3 hook variations of it.** Launch them in the same campaign. That's your cheapest path to a second winner.
>
> **5. Add a kill rule and a scale rule to every test**, *before* launching. Decisions made with data in advance beat emotional decisions later."

### Part B: The "Aha Moment" Reframe (15 sec)

> "The big shift is this: **stop asking 'is this a good ad?' and start asking 'what did Meta spend on, and why?'** Once you do that, creative stops being a guessing game and becomes a **system**: research, test, read, iterate."

### Part C: The Soft Authority CTA (20–30 sec)

**Rules:** No hard sell. No urgency tricks. Position as a peer who runs this system. Make the next step easy and optional.

**Option 1: Subtle and Neutral**
> "That's the process I run on every account I work with, focused on scaling cold traffic profitably on Meta. If this was useful, feel free to use it as is. And if you'd like me to run the same breakdown on **your** top ads and show you where the next winners are hiding, just reply to this message and I'll put one together."

**Option 2: Slightly More Direct (Warm Outreach)**
> "I made this using one of our own ad accounts, but I'd happily do the same teardown on yours. Reply with 'send it' and I'll record a breakdown of your best and worst ads, same format, no strings."

**Option 3: Authority-Forward (Inbound or Referral)**
> "I'm [Name]. I'm a media buyer and creative strategist who works on scaling cold traffic on Meta for DTC brands. If you ever want a second set of eyes on your creative pipeline or CBO structure, my contact info is below. Either way, I hope the framework helps."

### Closing Line (all versions)

> "Thanks for watching. Go sort your campaign by spend."

**Why this works:** The last line is a micro-action, not a pitch. They end the video **doing** the thing you taught.

## Appendix B: Analysis Cheat Sheets

### B1. Metric → Funnel Stage → Likely Problem → Fix

| Metric | Funnel Stage | If it's low... | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spend share** | Meta's overall verdict | Meta doesn't see enough signal | Diagnose the stages below |
| **Hook rate** (3s plays ÷ impressions) | Scroll-stop | Weak first 3 seconds, unclear audience, no pattern interrupt | New hook, same body |
| **Hold rate** (ThruPlay ÷ 3s plays) | Attention / message | Slow pacing, mechanism too late, off-angle body | Tighten edit, move mechanism and proof earlier |
| **CTR (link)** | Click motivation | Weak CTA, no curiosity gap, unclear next step | Rework CTA, add reason to click |
| **CPM** | Auction / relevance | Low engagement signal or narrow audience | Improve creative engagement, broaden |
| **Click → Purchase CVR** | Landing page / offer | Message mismatch, weak offer, slow page | Align ad ↔ landing page; test offer |
| **CPA / ROAS** | Profitability | Any of the above | Fix the earliest broken stage first |

> **Diagnostic rule:** Always fix the **earliest** broken stage in the funnel first. A great landing page cannot save an ad nobody watches.

### B2. Hook Type Library (for naming the `[Hook Type]` on screen)

| Hook Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Problem callout** | Opens with the pain: "If you're tired of ___" |
| **Call-out audience** | Names the viewer: "If you're a [persona]..." |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Unexpected visual or sound that breaks the scroll |
| **Bold claim** | Strong, specific promise up front |
| **Curiosity gap** | Opens a loop the viewer needs closed |
| **Social proof first** | Leads with a result, review, or number |
| **Demo-first** | Shows the product doing the thing in 2 seconds |
| **Us vs. them / comparison** | Positions against the category default |
| **Contrarian** | Challenges a common belief |

### B3. Awareness-Level Quick Reference (cold traffic)

| Level | Viewer thinks | Angle should... |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | "I don't have a problem." | Dramatize or reveal the problem |
| **Problem-aware** | "I have a problem, no solution yet." | Mirror the problem, introduce the solution category |
| **Solution-aware** | "I know solutions exist." | Differentiate with a unique mechanism |
| **Product-aware** | "I know you, but I'm unsure." | Proof, comparison, objection handling |
| **Most aware** | "I'm ready to buy." | Offer, urgency, simplicity (usually retargeting) |

### B4. The 4-Lens Quick Check (screenshot-able)

```
HOOK       → Does it stop the scroll in 3 seconds? (Hook rate)
ANGLE      → One-sentence message, right awareness level?
MECHANISM  → Is there a believable "why this works"?
PACING     → Does attention hold? (Retention curve)
```

### B5. Iteration Decision Tree

```
Did the ad get meaningful spend?
├── NO  → Fix the HOOK first (hook rate, first frame, audience callout). Relaunch.
└── YES → Is it profitable?
          ├── YES → WINNER → iterate: hooks → angles → formats. Protect and extend.
          └── NO  → Where did the funnel break?
                    ├── Low hold rate → fix pacing / mechanism timing
                    ├── Low CTR       → fix CTA / curiosity / next-step clarity
                    └── Low CVR       → fix ad ↔ landing page match / offer
```

## Appendix C: Delivery and Loom Best Practices

### Recording Setup
- **Screen + face cam bubble** (bottom-left). Face cam builds trust, so keep it on.
- **Close unrelated tabs and notifications.** Use a clean browser profile.
- **Blur sensitive data** (billing, other clients' names) before recording. Respect NDAs.
- **Prep tabs in order:** (1) Face cam title slide / ad thumbnail → (2) Ads Manager sorted by spend → (3) Pipeline diagram → (4) Ad playback + metrics → (5) Iteration doc → (6) Checklist slide.

### Delivery
- **Speak slightly slower than normal.** Use deliberate pauses on key numbers.
- **Point with the cursor** to whatever you are describing. Never talk about something that is not on screen.
- **Use plain language.** If you say a term (e.g., "hold rate"), define it in 5 words the first time.
- **Clarity over hype.** No exclamation-mark energy. Calm confidence reads as expertise.
- **Cut filler** with Loom's trim tool. Remove long pauses, "um," and false starts.

### Loom Packaging
- **Title:** `[Brand/Vertical] Ad Breakdown: Why This $[X] Ad [Won / Failed] and What We're Doing Next`
- **Thumbnail:** The ad's first frame + the spend number.
- **Description:** 3 bullets: (1) what the video covers, (2) timestamps, (3) one-line invitation.
- **Chapters / timestamps:** Match the run-of-show table above.
- **Call to action in Loom:** Pin a single CTA button (reply / book a call).

### The "Aha Moment" Test (run before sending)
Ask yourself: *"If the viewer never talks to me, did they still learn something they can use tomorrow?"*
If the answer is **no**, cut the fluff and add more concrete insight. If **yes**, send it.
